We are pleased to invite you and/or your representatives to the [BY-COVID project](https://by-covid.org/) <strong>Workshop on Integration of individual-level socioeconomic data for infectious diseases research and prevention in Europe</strong>. 

The BY-COVID project aims to make data on COVID-19 and other infectious diseases available to scientists and the wider public. This workshop is part of the BY-COVID project's Work Package 5 implementing Use Cases, and is co-organised by [KNAW-DANS](https://dans.knaw.nl/nl/)/[CESSDA](https://www.cessda.eu/), [Sciensano](https://www.sciensano.be/en) and [IACS](https://www.iacs.es/). 

The workshop will take place online on 6th of June, 09:30 - 12:00 and will be recorded to access afterwards. 

**Keywords**: Real-world Data, Social Sciences, Data Integration, Federated Research,  Vaccine Effectiveness, Pandemic Preparedness


## Scope of the workshop


The <strong>baseline use case</strong>, developed in the BY-COVID Demonstrator Project (WP5), is <strong>prototyping a workflow standard for population health research</strong>. The workflow provides a structured process for <strong>causal inference based</strong> on <strong>real-world observational data</strong> to respond to policy-relevant questions. 

Conducting causal research, which often implies the need for detailed individual-level data to mitigate confounding across national borders, brings challenges in terms of <strong>sensitive data access</strong> and <strong>interoperability</strong>. This <strong>federated analysis workflow</strong> is designed to leverage real-world heterogeneous data sources from different domains in multiple countries in a privacy-preserving and interoperable way, using valuable and innovative technologies like Directed Acyclic Graphs, synthetic data, and containerisation. A detailed description of the proposed framework has been described by [Meurisse & Estupiñán‑Romero et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-023-02068-3).

A policy-relevant research question on <strong>real-world vaccine effectiveness</strong> has been defined and is used to demonstrate the implementation of the described framework. More specifically, we aim to investigate the real-world effectiveness of SARS-CoV-2 primary vaccination as compared to partial or no vaccination in preventing SARS-CoV-2 infection in resident populations spanning different countries. For this, we have designed an observational study to <strong>emulate the hypothetical target trial</strong> to estimate the causal effect of interest. Details on the methodology can be consulted in the published [study protocol](https://zenodo.org/records/7680225#.ZAX-Jh_MKUk).

Further developments in the baseline use case will address how to <strong>integrate additional data types</strong>. Here, we will focus on those data sources containing information on individual-level <strong>socioeconomic status (SES)</strong> originating from <strong>Social Sciences</strong>. The integration of socioeconomic data into health research has gained significant attention in recent years, acknowledging that social determinants can play a crucial role in shaping health outcomes. A first <strong>Workshop</strong> was held in April 2023 on the <i>“Integration of socioeconomic data in observational studies on vaccine effectiveness”</i> to promote these developments and stimulate community discussion. The [workshop report](https://zenodo.org/doi/10.5281/zenodo.7985916) has been published and forms the basis of the current work. 

The current <strong>workshop aims</strong> (1) to identify solutions for integrating socioeconomic data in population health research; (2) generalise such solutions in various disciplinary and geographical contexts (EU-level focus); and (3) translate the workshop findings into an innovative workflow standard to federated population health research. 

Participants will have the chance to familiarise with the project and socioeconomic data requirements, mobilisation, and integration for infectious disease research, and collaborate on these issues in breakout sessions together with experts from social and health science backgrounds.  Please refer to the agenda for more details (see below). 

## Audience

This workshop targets researchers working with sensitive data and having experience with or being interested in solutions towards the integration of individual-level socio-economic data for policy-relevant infectious diseases research. This can include BY-COVID partners or anyone interested in the topic. Due to the interactive nature of the workshop, places are limited.
